Full figure captions from report.tex (before shortening), in figure order.

Figure 1 - throughput.png (label Throughput)
Throughput in million element matrices per second versus the number of elements $N$, in float and double, on the CPU (AMD EPYC 9454P, 48 cores, OpenMP) and the GPU (Tesla T4, CUDA), with LayoutLeft (solid) and LayoutRight (dashed). Each point is the average over $\min(1000, 10^8/N)$ kernel runs after one untimed warm-up run. For small $N$ the fixed cost of a kernel launch dominates (about 4\,$\mu$s on the GPU and 14\,$\mu$s on the CPU), so the throughput grows in proportion to $N$. At large $N$ the fastest GPU configuration (float, LayoutLeft) reaches 1890\,MElem/s and the fastest CPU configuration (float, LayoutRight) 2163\,MElem/s; in double both reach half of that (946 and 1082\,MElem/s). The higher peaks at intermediate $N$ (4397\,MElem/s on the GPU at $N = 38\,629$, about 3900\,MElem/s on the CPU around $N = 10^6$) occur while $J$ and $A$ still fit in cache, see Figure~\ref{Bandwidth}.

Figure 2 - bandwidth.png (label Bandwidth)
Memory throughput of the kernel, counting the 25 values moved per element (9 reads of $J$ and 16 writes of $A$), on the CPU (left) and the T4 (right). Dashed lines: theoretical DRAM peak, 460.8\,GB/s for the 12 DDR5-4800 channels of the EPYC and 320\,GB/s for the GDDR6 of the T4. Dotted lines: the $N$ at which $J$ and $A$ together fill the last-level cache (304\,MB of L2 + L3 on the CPU, 4\,MB of L2 on the T4). To the left of these lines the repeated kernel runs read their data from cache, so the apparent bandwidth exceeds the DRAM peak (up to 774\,GB/s on the CPU and 440\,GB/s on the T4). Beyond the cache every configuration settles at a bandwidth that is the same for float and double: about 216\,GB/s on the CPU (47\,\% of peak, or about 77\,\% when the extra reads caused by writing $A$ through the cache are counted) and 189\,GB/s on the T4 with LayoutLeft (59\,\% of peak). Float therefore processes twice as many elements per second as double, the signature of a memory-bound kernel.

Figure 3 - gflops.png (label GFlops)
Floating point throughput, counting 68 flops per element as written in the kernel (9 for $C_0$--$C_2$, 5 for the determinant, 1 division, 36 for $G_0$--$G_5$, 9 for the three negated sums and 8 for $A_{33}$). With a fixed number of flops per element the curves have the same shape as in Figure~\ref{Throughput}. At large $N$ the T4 reaches 129\,GFLOP/s in float and 64\,GFLOP/s in double (LayoutLeft), and the CPU 147 and 74\,GFLOP/s (LayoutRight). That is only 1.6\,\% of the T4's 8.1\,TFLOP/s float peak and about 3.5\,\% of the EPYC's estimated 4.2\,TFLOP/s, so computation is not what limits the kernel.

Figure 4 - roofline.png (label Roofline)
Roofline model of the T4 (left) and the EPYC (right). Solid and dashed lines: the attainable performance in float and double, the minimum of peak bandwidth $\times$ arithmetic intensity and peak compute (T4: 8.1\,TFLOP/s float from the data sheet, 253\,GFLOP/s double at 1/32 rate; EPYC: estimated as 48 cores $\times$ 2.75\,GHz $\times$ 16 double-precision flops per cycle $\approx$ 2.1\,TFLOP/s, twice that in float). The kernel's arithmetic intensity, $68 / (25 \cdot 4) = 0.68$ flop/byte in float and 0.34 flop/byte in double (shaded band), lies far below the ridge points (T4: 25.3 float, 0.79 double; EPYC: 9.2 float, 4.6 double), so both devices are limited by memory bandwidth in both precisions. From the data sheet the T4 should reach $320\,\text{GB/s} / 100\,\text{B} = 3201$\,MElem/s (218\,GFLOP/s) in float; we measure 1890\,MElem/s (filled markers, $N = 4\cdot10^7$), 59\,\% of the prediction, and 946 of 1600\,MElem/s in double. The EPYC reaches 2163 of 4608 and 1082 of 2304\,MElem/s (47\,\%). Hollow markers: the best in-cache measurements, which lie above the DRAM roof. Performance can only be improved by moving fewer bytes, for example by computing $J$ from the vertex coordinates inside the kernel or by storing only the 10 distinct entries of the symmetric $A$ (19 instead of 25 values per element).

Figure 5 - layout.png (label Layout)
Throughput with LayoutLeft divided by throughput with LayoutRight; above 1 LayoutLeft is faster. On the T4, LayoutLeft is 1.4--1.5$\times$ faster at large $N$ and up to 5$\times$ faster while the data fits in L2: with LayoutLeft the element index is the fastest-varying index, so the 32 threads of a warp, which handle consecutive elements, access consecutive addresses and their loads and stores coalesce into full memory transactions, whereas with LayoutRight neighbouring threads are 9 ($J$) or 16 ($A$) values apart. On the CPU each thread processes a contiguous block of elements, and LayoutRight, which keeps the 25 values of an element together, is up to 3$\times$ faster for $N \approx 10^4$--$10^5$ and equally fast (ratio 0.95) at large $N$, where both layouts stream from DRAM. The drop of the CPU ratio to 0.7 at $N = 4\cdot10^7$ coincides with a LayoutLeft stride $N \cdot \texttt{sizeof(Number)}$ that is a multiple of 4\,KiB, which likely makes the 25 data streams compete for the same cache sets. Recommended layouts are therefore LayoutLeft on the GPU and LayoutRight on the CPU, which are also the Kokkos defaults for CUDA and host memory.

Figure 6 - transfers.png (label Transfers)
Data transfers between host and T4. Left: time of one kernel run (LayoutLeft) compared with copying the Jacobians $J$ to the device and the element matrices $A$ back to the host once. Right: the effective host--device bandwidth of these copies. Small copies are dominated by latency (25--50\,$\mu$s); large copies reach 10.1\,GB/s host to device and 12.6\,GB/s device to host, about what the T4's PCIe 3.0 $\times$16 link delivers. At $N = 4\cdot10^7$ the two copies take 346\,ms in float and 690\,ms in double, 16$\times$ the kernel time (21 and 42\,ms). For the copies to take no longer than the kernel, the link would have to sustain about 189\,GB/s, the kernel's own device-memory bandwidth and about 15$\times$ more than PCIe delivers here. On the CPU the host mirror and the device view share memory, so the copies there are no-ops and are not shown.
